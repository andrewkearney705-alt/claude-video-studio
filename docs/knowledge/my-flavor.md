# Andrew's flavor: how a borrowed idea becomes his

When Andrew wants a reel "like that one," Claude never copies it. It keeps the parts that drove the result (from the breakdown's "likely drivers") and rebuilds everything else in Andrew's own signature. This file is that signature. Only Andrew's explicit choices and proven results change it (see `../../CURATION.md`).

## Who's on screen
- Brand line: "I don't code. I make AI do real work, and I show you how."
- Credibility: AI Delivery Lead at a Fortune 500 insurer, SCAD Advertising & Branding grad, Southeast Emmy for marketing a cooking show, working photographer and videographer.
- Real life that can show up: building tools with Claude, cooking from scratch, hiking, climbing, skiing, travel.
- Never: his employer's name, tools, colleagues or numbers; politics; AI news roundups or "10 tools" lists.

## His look (approved styles)
- **A · Light desk**, **B · Dark editorial**, **C · Set reveal**, plus split-screen for explainers. Full-frame alternation approved (v3).
- Taste: engaging but refined and professional. v1 was "a bit much." Grade on him and sound matter most.
- His edge over phone-camera creators is cinematography: use it (letterbox montage, real lenses, motivated light) wherever a reference was shot flat.

## Remix recipe (run this for every "make one like that")
1. **Pull the drivers** from the reference's breakdown (`elements.md`). Those are the only parts carried over, and only in structure (e.g. "trend-sound supercut hook," "result shown in frame 1"), never their words, footage or likeness.
2. **Swap the content**: his real project, footage or story, from the board or the Footage Scout.
3. **Apply his look** above for everything that wasn't a driver: setting, grade, type, sound.
4. **Check against what's proven for him** (`patterns.md`, "Proven for Andrew"), which outranks anything proven for other creators.
5. **Offer 3 hook versions** so the Trial Lab can test them.
6. Say in one line what was borrowed and what's his.
