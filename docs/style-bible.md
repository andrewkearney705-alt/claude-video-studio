# Short-Form Style Bible

What each creator actually does on screen, from a frame-by-frame review (0.07–0.2s steps through hooks, 1–5s steps through full videos) of their top reels, Oct 30 2026. "Free match" = the closest free font Claude uses to recreate the look.

---

## 1. Roberto Nickson (@rpn) — "the polished tech storyteller"

**Tone & voice**
- Talks like a friend who just saw something wild: "Yeah, if it wasn't for the live audience, I don't think anyone would believe it." Starts mid-thought, no greeting, no "hey guys."
- Reactions are part of the script: "WHAT?!", "Huge deal", "Wtf… Google cooked."
- Each video ends on stakes or a question, never a summary. His post caption repeats the full script as bullet facts (Tesla post: 10 bullet specs), so the caption is a second piece of content.
- Long-form essays switch to a quiet, reflective register ("Create to serve… Fall in love with the journey.").

**Frame & layout**
- Explainers: 50/50 split from frame 1. Top = B-roll (official launch footage, his own prop shots, screen recordings). Bottom = him at the desk.
- Breaks the split every 10–15s: full-screen him, full-screen B-roll (keyboard close-up, robot in his studio, phone in hand), full-screen title card on black.
- Series (Agent Wars): full-frame vlog, ultra-wide 0.5x lens, low angles, handheld.
- Film essays: full-frame portraits and found footage, warm and dark.

**Text system (4 tiers)**
| Tier | What | Look | Free match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook title | 2 lines over the top half: small line + big line ("GOOGLE LAUNCHES NEW / FRONTIER MODEL") | All caps, ultra-wide extended sans, white, no box | Unbounded / Syncopate Bold |
| Brand wordmark | Uses the product's own logo style ("RORDSTER" in Tesla-like type) + italic subline "CRAZIEST TECH DEMO OF ALL TIME?" | Mirrors the brand being covered | Orbitron / Michroma for tech brands |
| Running captions | 1–3 words, sitting exactly on the split seam | Small, all caps, monospace, white, letter-spaced | Space Mono / IBM Plex Mono |
| Keyword promotion | The important word jumps out of the mono captions into a display font, one per 5–10s | Changes font every time: thin spaced serif ("FRONTIER"), script + tall condensed stack ("Huge" over "DEAL"), elegant serif ("ARGON", "SIMULATION", "Design", "Top 5 AI models") | Instrument Serif, Great Vibes + Bebas Neue, Playfair Display |
| Labels | Small rounded pill on screenshots ("BENCHMARKS", "DEEP") | White text on translucent grey pill | Inter Medium |
| Film essay subtitles | Tiny italic serif in gold/yellow, centered lower third ("dead.", "There's no social any…") | Cinematic subtitle | Cormorant Garamond Italic |
| Word-slam | One huge wide white word per face cut ("SOCIAL" / "MEDIA" / "IS") | Extended bold sans, center | Unbounded Black |

**How text animates**
- Captions: hard cut on, word-timed to the syllable. No fade, no bounce. ("IT'S A" → "Huge" appears at 5.18s → "DEAL" stacks under it at 5.45s, each on its spoken beat.)
- Hook titles: slide in from the side with a heavy horizontal motion smear (2–3 frames of streaked letters), hold, then smear out the same way when the shot changes.
- Keyword stacks build one word at a time on the beat.
- Agent Wars titles: one serif word per shot ("Design" → "Engineer" → "3d print" → "Bridge"), about 0.5s each, cut on the beat.

**Other overlays**
- App-icon stickers: the AI company logos (Claude, OpenAI, Meta, Kimi, Grok) pop in one after another around his head, rounded-square icons, 0.1s stagger.
- Hand-drawn white arrow from a title to the thing on screen ("Top 5 AI models" → monitor).
- Contestant card, top-left: brand name bold + logo icon, model name small underneath ("OpenAI / GPT 6 Astra (High)").
- Custom scoreboard card ("Model Strength Index"): light grey card on grid background, bars per model, the current one highlighted; returns several times as it updates.
- Big counters in the middle ("20 pounds", "26.5 pounds").
- Screenshots and screen recordings with rounded corners.

**Transitions & camera**
- Hard cuts on words; occasional whip-blur in.
- Glitchy TV-static and "screen-on" effects when he uses a CRT TV prop: static → logo glitches in → glowing ring around a "4" (he composites content onto the prop's screen).
- Match cut for reveals: the same stair angle, empty warehouse → finished studio (13.6M reel).
- Subtle punch-ins on the talking head.

**Color & set**
- Dark studio, warm skin, cool blue/purple monitors and practical lamps behind, white round paper lantern as a key light in frame. Wardrobe: dark sweater or utility jacket. Handheld dynamic mic as a prop.
- Film essays: warm, crushed blacks, grain.

**Sound** (measured)
- Music bed never drops. 30–50 sound hits per minute on explainers. Only 0–2 pauses over 0.15s in a whole minute.

## 2. Greg LaVecchia (@greglav) — "the founder in the wild"

**Tone & voice**
- Blunt, confessional, a little cocky, always about ambition: "some of you think you're too cool…", "I have spent 100's of millions of dollars on…", "you can't build an extraordinary life with ordinary effort." Swears for emphasis ("f*cking stars").
- All post captions are one lowercase line, no hashtags or just 3–4.

**Frame & layout**
- Full-frame 9:16 at 1440x2560, handheld, mostly one continuous take, walking toward the camera.
- Location is the story: a Bloom-wrapped delivery truck on a NYC street (567K), a red-lit brand party (235K, 347K), a garage gym full of supercars, a trade-show booth with a wall of cans.
- Product is always in his hand or in the frame.

**Text system**
| Tier | Look | Free match |
|---|---|---|
| Pinned headline | All lowercase, 2 short lines, white semibold sans, no box, small (about 3% of frame height), top-centre, stays the entire video ("some of you think / you're too cool") | Instagram-native text / Inter SemiBold |
| Captions | Sentence case, 3–6 words per line, 2 lines max, white, mid-frame; sometimes yellow | Inter SemiBold |
| Montage words | 1–2 words, bold sans at ~85% opacity so the image shows through ("f*cking stars", "Never take") | Inter Bold, 85% opacity |
| Quote card | Handwritten white marker, key word capitalised and underlined ("doubt is cured / by Action") | Caveat Brush / Permanent Marker |

**How text animates**
- Pinned headline: no animation at all. It is there from frame 1 to the end.
- Captions: hard cuts by phrase.
- Quote card: small at first, then a body-wipe (someone's shoulder passes the lens) cuts to a wide shot where the text is huge; later the text sits **behind** him (he walks in front of it).

**Other overlays**
- A proof card pops onto the frame for 2–3s: a screenshot of Google search volumes, a news headline about "600,000,000 cans" held over his head as a sign.

**Transitions & camera**
- One-takes: none. Montage: opens on 2–3 colour-inverted (negative) frames that snap to normal on the first beat, whip-blur transitions, speed ramps, shots of 0.5–1.2s.

**Color**
- Warm, rich, filmic: red and amber at night, golden daylight, clean whites in the gym.

**Sound**
- One-takes: his voice + quiet music. Montage: voice-over line chopped across shots over a driving track.

**What fails**
- Seated, static, neutral room (41K, his lowest).

## 3. The Diary of a CEO (@thediaryofaceopodcast) — "the authority clip"

**Tone & voice**
- Grave and high-stakes. Every hook is a question or a warning: "THE WORLD ORDER IS COLLAPSING…", "WORLD WAR 3 IS COMING?", "IS TRUMP LYING ABOUT THIS?"
- Post caption: 3–5 sentence summary of the guest's argument in neutral journalist voice, credential named, ends with "Comment 'watch' and we'll personally send you this episode!"

**Frame & layout**
- 0–4.5s: two stacked shots. Host on top (silent, skeptical stare), guest on bottom.
- ~5s: cut to full-frame guest. From then on: guest close-ups, host reaction close-ups, data/motion-graphic inserts.
- Dark set, bookshelves, cinematic shallow depth of field, slow push-ins.

**Text system**
| Tier | Look |
|---|---|
| Question box | White rounded rectangle, black all-caps bold condensed sans, 2 lines, tight leading, centred on the seam between the two shots (Free match: Inter Black / Archivo Black) |
| Credential tag | Small white bold text next to the guest + a tiny hand-drawn curved arrow pointing at them ("WWIII Threat Professor"), appears at ~2s |
| Show badge | Top-left: light box with "DOAC" in black bold + guest name in small white bold below. On every frame. |
| Captions | One word at a time, lowercase, white bold sans, centred low-middle; emotional words in yellow/orange |
| Data | Big numbers in motion graphics ("+4.5%", "3.91M"), country maps lit in red/gold, a globe, scanned documents |

**How text animates**
- Question box: no animation. On at frame 1, off at ~4.5s.
- Credential tag + arrow: hard on at ~2s.
- Captions: hard cut per word.

**Sound**
- Clean dialogue, low tension music, few SFX (10–16 hits per minute).

## 4. Rajan Chida (@chida.rajan) — "build in public with a friend"

**Tone & voice**: casual, story-first, "me and my boy", money-transparent ("bought 2.5 acres for $37,000").
**Frame**: tripod wide shots in found locations (an empty university lecture hall as a studio), drone shots of the land, whiteboard explainers, selfie bursts.
**Text**: 2–3 word captions, lowercase, white bold sans with a thin black outline, placed **above his head** in the upper third. Money amounts highlighted in **green**. One-off all-caps shout words ("NUMBERS!"). Whiteboard handwriting for the maths (boxed "$300,000").
**Animation**: hard cuts by phrase.
**Sound**: constant music + lots of hits (39–89 per minute).

## 5. Chase Ridgeway (@chasesdayinthelife) — "the relatable episode"

**Tone & voice**: self-deprecating, wholesome, twist endings ("April fools. I'm still single.").
**Frame**: static tripod wides; he walks through frame; physical comedy; big reaction faces.
**Text**: small white native-style text. Opening premise as 2 lines for ~7s ("My Tuesday night as a 23 year old (I finally got a girlfriend)"). Then a timestamp + action label per scene ("4:56pm / Work is over", "7:33pm / Get dressed for dinner").
**Animation**: none; text changes with each scene.
**Sound**: music-led, little or no voice.

## Cross-creator rules that held up
1. **Every top performer has a text system with at least two tiers** (a hook headline + running captions). The fonts differ; the hierarchy does not.
2. **Text appears with hard cuts on the spoken word.** Nobody uses bouncy pop-in animations; smears, wipes and stacking are the only motion.
3. **Captions are small.** Roberto's and DOAC's running captions are about 2.5–4% of frame height; the drama comes from the occasional giant word.
4. **One signature overlay per creator**: Roberto's display-font keyword, Greg's pinned lowercase headline, DOAC's white question box, Chida's green money, Chase's timestamps, Rui Fu's gold headline. Andrew should pick one and keep it on every video.
5. **The location or prop is the thumbnail**: CRT TV, Bloom truck, empty lecture hall, warehouse.
6. **Series familiarity beats novelty** (Kallaway on Nate Gaffney): keep one visual setup across a series; viewers see thousands of videos between yours, so repetition reads as a brand, not a rerun.
7. **Name the viewer** (identity labels) and **match your delivery to the audience you want** (demeanor matching).
8. **Comment keywords win comments, not always reach**: teardown and tutorial reels with a giveaway pull 25-40% comment rates; pair them with "wow" posts for reach.
